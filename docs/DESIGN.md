# SpeechMonster practice journey

SpeechMonster is the working visible brand for this draft. The repository, URL, existing browser storage keys and local progress schema retain their identities. This is not a trademark-clearance claim.

## From controls to a small useful practice

The home offers one primary starting path: practice one section without a microphone. A labeled educational sample lets someone try the interaction before editing their own script. The user can also bring a script or choose a whole-talk take. The free local feature set remains available; no paid AI service is represented as live.

Setup shows the chosen section, time budget and amount of support. Prompt choices, optional shorter routes, plan editing and saved progress appear when needed. Practice is a quiet surface with a timer, script or cues, Help and manual marks. Pausing stops the timer and explains how to resume. Finishing acknowledges a real completed take, then asks for a self-check and offers one next drill. “Done for now” is a legitimate finish. Saving and exporting remain explicit.

Recording and slides open on request; the full Studio remains available for teleprompter, cue cards, slide layouts, recording playback and downloads. Navigation cannot hide an active practice. The generated companion is absent during active or paused practice and recording. Quiet mode removes the characters; reduced-motion preferences remove nonessential transitions. Text communicates every state independently of illustration.

## Research translated into behavior

The supplied UX research uses Granola as a reference for editable human decisions, source inspection, and an expressive identity around a quiet working surface. Here, sections, key ideas, verbatim protected facts and optional passages are user-editable; practice results retain immutable plan versions. Transcript wording is inspectable and is distinguished from recollection. A recognition failure is not evidence of definite omission.

The Headspace reference contributes a manageable first step and a gentle return, rather than a copied therapeutic relationship. One section is a valid practice. There are no streaks, guilt mechanics, personality judgments, emotion detection or mastery percentages. The companion gives existing guidance a recognizable place; it is not an AI chat service.

Wyatt Feaster’s [supplied psychology discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MVOpq3WRnbo) informs hierarchy, bounded choice, visible progress and the completion moment. The Choose / Practice / Reflect sequence and actual timer/marks show where the user is. They do not estimate learning mastery. Download progress reflects real bytes and worker messages describe real processing. There is no artificial delay, fictional analysis stage, or claim that the redesign improves an app “10x.” These transfers are design hypotheses, not product-specific psychological findings.

## Original character assets

Six original, generated transparent illustrations form a cohesive cast. The lilac companion has welcome, listening and completion poses. Supporting yellow, coral and mint characters appear in preparation and optional supporting surfaces. They never carry an error or instruction alone.

The source illustrations were visually inspected. WebP delivery is lossless: decoded RGBA pixels, dimensions and alpha match each original PNG exactly. Only image pixels are carried into the public assets; Library metadata, private paths and transfer credentials are excluded. Asset SHA-256 hashes and byte sizes are recorded in `scripts/art-hashes.json` and verified by the audit.

## Evaluation boundaries

The browser checks cover the complete synthetic practice journey, pause/recovery, self-check/drill, versioning, explicit progress export/save/reload/clear, protected shorter routes, offline manual practice, recording/transcription regressions, quiet mode, reduced motion and narrow viewport reflow. Automated accessibility checks include contrast and semantic rules on sampled screens. They do not establish full accessibility conformance or physical mobile behavior.

Evaluate the next iteration with people: can they anticipate the first action, find and correct a fact, recover a pause or failed capture, distinguish self-check from STT, and stop or return without pressure? Do not treat visual preference as recognition accuracy or learning improvement.
